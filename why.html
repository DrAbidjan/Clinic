Why records matter

A record is never just paperwork

What a forensic dentist wants every clinic and hospital to know.

Written by Abid Ali, Dentist & Forensic Odontologist

A record can protect a patient

Allergies, blood thinners, pregnancy, heart conditions. When these are written down and shown before treatment starts, a mistake is stopped before it happens. A record that is missing, unreadable or in someone else's drawer protects nobody.

A record can protect a dentist

If a patient disagrees later about what was done, what was agreed or what was said, the clinic's record is the first thing anyone asks for. Dated notes, a signed consent and a clear treatment plan show exactly what happened and why.

A record can help a family find answers

In forensic odontology, teeth and dental records help identify people when nothing else can. In international disaster-victim identification, dental findings are one of the primary ways of confirming who someone was. Those answers are only possible because a dentist wrote down the chart, took the X-rays and kept them.

Good records are not extra work. They are the work, written down. ClinicRecords.pk is made so that writing them takes less time than not writing them.

What a good record contains

  • Who the patient is, and how to reach them
  • Medical history and medical alerts
  • A dated tooth chart and treatment plan
  • Clinical notes for every visit
  • X-rays and photographs
  • Consent, prescriptions and payments
